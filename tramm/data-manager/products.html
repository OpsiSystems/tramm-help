Product Groups & Products

Product Groups categorise goods and set shared rules. Products define the physical specs — size, weight, and loading behaviour.

Tramm uses two related entities for managing goods: Product Groups and Products. A Product Group is a classification that sets shared rules for a category of goods — which vehicles can carry them, which sites can handle them, and how much quantity variance is acceptable. A Product is the specific item record, holding the physical facts about a single product: its dimensions, weight, and how fast it loads and unloads.

Unlike the Vehicle and Site class patterns, a Product can belong to more than one Product Group simultaneously — so a product can inherit rules from multiple categories at once.

Product Group — the shared rules
Defines the handling rules that apply to a category of goods.
  • Shared attributes for matching and filtering
  • Restrictions (vehicle types, site types)
  • Variance tolerance for catch-weight or bulk goods
  • A product can belong to multiple groups
Product — the physical specification
Defines the physical facts for a specific item.
  • Weight and volume per unit
  • Physical dimensions (L × W × H)
  • Loading and offloading rate
  • Stackability rules and limits

What is a Product Group?

A Product Group is a classification label for a category of goods. It holds the shared rules that apply to every product in the group — restrictions, attributes, and variance tolerance — so you do not have to duplicate those rules on every individual product.

Think of a Product Group like a goods specification category — a classification that defines the shared handling rules for a type of goods. It sets the attribute rules, vehicle and site restrictions, and acceptable quantity variance for all products in the group. Products then provide their own physical specification: dimensions, weight, loading speed, and stacking behaviour.

Unlike Vehicle Classes and Site Classes, a Product can belong to more than one Product Group simultaneously. A refrigerated item that also carries hazard classification can belong to both a "Frozen Goods" group and a "Hazardous Materials" group at the same time — inheriting the rules and restrictions of both.

Product Group Fields

Fields marked Required must be filled in before saving. All other fields are optional.

Field Description Req Opt
Name The display name of the group. Should describe the product category clearly — for example, Frozen Goods, Hazardous Materials, or Bulk Liquids. ✓
Attributes Custom attributes shared by all products in this group. Used for matching and filtering in the Planner. ✓
Restrictions Rules that control which vehicle types or site types can handle products in this group. Example: only refrigerated vehicles can carry products in the Frozen Goods group.
Restrictions are applied via AttributeRules — define the attribute conditions a vehicle or site must meet.
 ✓
Variance Tolerance How much quantity variance is acceptable for products in this group. Set a Kind (Value or Percentage), an Under amount, and an Over amount. Useful for bulk or catch-weight goods where exact quantities can vary.
Kind: None disables tolerance checking. Kind: Value sets an absolute threshold. Kind: Percentage sets a relative threshold.
 ✓

What is a Product?

A Product is the physical goods specification — the record that holds the exact dimensions, weight, and loading behaviour of a specific item in your catalogue.

Think of a Product as a product datasheet — the physical specification for a specific goods item. Where a Product Group sets the rules (what can carry it, what variance is allowed), the Product holds the physical facts: how big it is, how heavy, how fast it loads, and whether anything can be stacked on top of it.

3D Visualiser requires physical dimensions. If you use the 3D Visualiser in the Planner, you must set the physical dimensions (Length, Width, Height) and weight on each Product. Without dimensions, the 3D Visualiser cannot represent the product visually.

Unlike Vehicle Classes and Site Classes, a Product can belong to more than one Product Group. A single product that is both refrigerated and hazardous can belong to both the "Frozen Goods" and "Hazardous Materials" groups — inheriting restrictions and attributes from both simultaneously.

Product Fields

Fields marked Required must be filled in before saving. Fields marked Recommended are not required by the system but are practically necessary for accurate planning.

Field Description Req Rec Opt
Name The display name of the product as it appears in orders and planning. ✓
Code Short unique identifier for this product. Used in imports, integrations, and order line items. ✓
Product Group(s) The group or groups this product belongs to. A product can belong to multiple groups — for example, both Frozen Goods and Hazardous Materials. Without a group, no shared restrictions or attributes apply to this product.
Recommended — products without a group will not inherit any handling restrictions or shared attributes.
 ✓
Size — Weight The weight of one unit of this product. Used by the Planner to check vehicle capacity against the total weight of a load. ✓
Size — Volume The volume of one unit. Used for capacity planning alongside weight — if the vehicle class has a volume limit, the Planner will check product volumes against it. ✓
Size — Dimensions (L × W × H) Physical dimensions of one unit: Length, Width, and Height. Required for the 3D Visualiser in the Planner to represent this product visually in a load plan.
If you use the 3D Visualiser, all three dimensions must be set — without them, the product will not appear in the visual load plan.
 ✓
Loading Rate How long it takes to load and offload this product, expressed in seconds per unit — LoadingSecondsPerUnit and OffloadingSecondsPerUnit. Used to calculate stop durations in the Planner.
If not set, the vehicle class or site loading rate applies as the fallback.
 ✓
Stackability Controls whether other products can be stacked on top of this product. Set IsDisabled to prevent stacking entirely, or define maximum surface loads per orientation — Upright, LengthWise, and WidthWise — each with an optional AllowRotation flag and a MaxLoad weight limit. Used by the 3D Visualiser. ✓

Adding a Product Group

Follow these steps to create a new Product Group in Data Manager. Set up your Product Groups before adding products so you can assign products to groups during setup.

  1. 1

    Open Data Manager and go to Product Groups

    From the main navigation, open Data Manager. Select Product Groups from the entity list on the left.

  2. 2

    Click Add

    Click the Add button in the toolbar. The new Product Group form will open.

  3. 3

    Enter a name that clearly describes the category

    Give the group a clear, descriptive name — for example, Frozen Goods, Bulk Liquids, or Hazardous Materials. The name appears in product records and in Planner filtering, so clarity helps.

  4. 4

    Add restrictions if applicable

    If only certain vehicle types or site types can handle this product category, add the appropriate restrictions here. For example, if only refrigerated vehicles can carry products in this group, set a vehicle restriction to match vehicles with a "refrigerated" attribute. Restrictions are defined as attribute rules.

  5. 5

    Add shared attributes if applicable

    If all products in this group share a set of attributes — for example, a "hazmat" flag used for matching — add those attributes here. Products in the group will inherit them automatically.

  6. 6

    Set a Variance Tolerance if this is a catch-weight or bulk product type

    If delivered quantities for this type of product typically vary from ordered quantities — for example, bulk products sold by approximate weight — set a Variance Tolerance. Choose a Kind (Value or Percentage), then set the acceptable Under and Over amounts. Leave Kind as None if exact quantities are always expected.

  7. 7

    Save

    Click Save. The Product Group is now available to assign to products.

Adding a Product

Follow these steps to add a new Product in Data Manager. Set up your Product Groups first so you can assign the product to a group during this process.

  1. 1

    Open Data Manager and go to Products

    From the main navigation, open Data Manager. Select Products from the entity list on the left.

  2. 2

    Click Add

    Click the Add button in the toolbar. The new Product form will open.

  3. 3

    Enter the product name and code

    Enter the product's display Name and a unique Code. The code is used in imports and order integrations, so it must match whatever identifier your order management system uses for this product.

  4. 4

    Assign the product to one or more Product Groups

    Select the Product Group or groups this product belongs to. A product can belong to multiple groups — for example, a product that is both frozen and hazardous should be assigned to both the Frozen Goods and Hazardous Materials groups. If no group applies, you can leave this blank, but the product will not inherit any shared restrictions or attributes.

  5. 5

    Enter the weight

    Enter the weight of one unit of this product. The Planner uses this figure to check whether a vehicle's weight capacity is exceeded by a given load. Accurate weights are essential for reliable capacity planning.

  6. 6

    Enter the volume if applicable

    If your operation plans against volume limits (cubic metres), enter the volume per unit here. If your vehicles are only capacity-limited by weight or units, you can leave volume blank.

  7. 7

    Enter physical dimensions if using the 3D Visualiser

    If your Tramm environment uses the 3D load visualiser, enter the product's Length, Width, and Height. These are required for the visualiser to draw the product in a load plan. If you are not using the 3D Visualiser, you can skip this step — though entering dimensions is good practice for future use.

  8. 8

    Set the Loading Rate if this product has a different loading speed

    If this product takes significantly longer — or shorter — to load or offload than your other products, enter the Loading Rate in seconds per unit. This affects how the Planner calculates stop duration. If you leave this blank, the vehicle class or site loading rate will be used as the default.

  9. 9

    Configure Stackability if needed

    If nothing can be placed on top of this product, set IsDisabled to prevent stacking entirely. If stacking is allowed but limited, define the maximum surface loads per orientation — Upright, LengthWise, and WidthWise — with the maximum load weight for each. The 3D Visualiser uses these settings when building a load plan. If stacking is unrestricted, you can leave this section blank.

  10. 10

    Save

    Click Save. The product is now available in the Planner and can be referenced in orders and load plans.

Tips & Common Mistakes

A few things to keep in mind when setting up Product Groups and Products for the first time.

  • Set up Product Groups before adding products. Even a single "General Goods" group is useful as a starting point for restrictions. Creating your groups first means you can assign products to them during initial setup rather than returning to each product later.
  • Enter dimensions carefully. The 3D Visualiser uses dimensions to draw the product in a load plan, so rough or approximate values lead to unrealistic load visualisations. Measure product dimensions from the outer packaging — the dimensions that matter are the space the product actually occupies in the vehicle.
  • A product can belong to multiple groups. Use this for products that have more than one set of handling requirements. A product assigned to both Frozen Goods and Hazardous Materials will inherit restrictions and attributes from both groups simultaneously — you do not need to duplicate rules.
  • Leaving dimensions blank when using the 3D Visualiser. If a product has no dimensions set, the 3D Visualiser cannot render it in the load plan — the product will simply not appear. This makes the visual load plan incomplete and unreliable. Always set Length, Width, and Height on every product you expect to see in the visualiser.
  • Setting a very tight Variance Tolerance on products that routinely vary in quantity. If the tolerance is narrower than the natural variation for a bulk or catch-weight product, the system will generate unnecessary warnings on deliveries. Set the tolerance to reflect the realistic variation you expect — not the theoretical ideal.
  • Forgetting that Loading Rate affects stop duration in the Planner. If a product takes a long time to load or unload and no loading rate is set anywhere — not on the product, the vehicle class, or the site — the Planner will underestimate stop times for orders that include this product. This leads to trips that run consistently late. If your products vary significantly in loading time, set the Loading Rate on the products that differ most from your average.
