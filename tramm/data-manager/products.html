Product Groups, Products & Transport Units

Product Groups categorise goods and set shared rules. Products and Transport Units define the physical specs — size, mass, and loading behaviour.

Tramm uses three related entities for managing goods: Product Groups, Products, and Transport Units. A Product Group is a classification that sets shared rules for a category of goods — which vehicles can carry them, which sites can handle them, and how much quantity variance is acceptable. A Product is the specific item record, holding the physical facts about a single product: its dimensions, mass, and how fast it loads and unloads. A Transport Unit is a physical packaging or handling unit (a rolltainer, a pallet) that behaves almost exactly like a Product but can also be packed with other Products — see What is a Transport Unit? below.

Unlike the Vehicle and Site class patterns, a Product can belong to more than one Product Group simultaneously — so a product can inherit rules from multiple categories at once.

Product Group — the shared rules
Defines the handling rules that apply to a category of goods.
  • Shared attributes for matching and filtering
  • Restrictions (vehicle types, site types)
  • Variance tolerance for catch-weight or bulk goods
  • A product can belong to multiple groups
Product — the physical specification
Defines the physical facts for a specific item.
  • Size — configurable Dimensions (mass, volume, L × W × H, etc.)
  • Loading and offloading rate
  • Stackability rules and limits

What is a Product Group?

A Product Group is a classification label for a category of goods. It holds the shared rules that apply to every product in the group — restrictions, attributes, and variance tolerance — so you do not have to duplicate those rules on every individual product.

Think of a Product Group like a goods specification category — a classification that defines the shared handling rules for a type of goods. It sets the attribute rules, vehicle and site restrictions, and acceptable quantity variance for all products in the group. Products then provide their own physical specification: dimensions, mass, loading speed, and stacking behaviour.

Unlike Vehicle Classes and Site Classes, a Product can belong to more than one Product Group simultaneously. A refrigerated item that also carries hazard classification can belong to both a "Frozen Goods" group and a "Hazardous Materials" group at the same time — inheriting the rules and restrictions of both.

Product Group Fields

Fields marked Required must be filled in before saving. All other fields are optional.

Field Description Req Opt
Name The display name of the group. Should describe the product category clearly — for example, Frozen Goods, Hazardous Materials, or Bulk Liquids. ✓
Icon A visual icon to represent this product group in the interface. Selected from a dropdown of available icons. Useful for quick visual identification when scanning product or order lists. ✓
Attributes Custom attributes shared by all products in this group. Used for matching and filtering in the Planner. ✓
Restrictions Rules that control which vehicle types or site types can handle products in this group. Example: only refrigerated vehicles can carry products in the Frozen Goods group.
Restrictions are applied via AttributeRules — define the attribute conditions a vehicle or site must meet.
 ✓
Variance Tolerance How much quantity variance is acceptable for products in this group. Set a Kind (Value or Percentage), an Under amount, and an Over amount. Useful for bulk or catch-weight goods where exact quantities can vary.
Kind: None disables tolerance checking. Kind: Value sets an absolute threshold. Kind: Percentage sets a relative threshold. Example: with a Value tolerance of 1 unit (under), loading 10 units but offloading 8 blocks commit and requires a variance reason; offloading 9 (within tolerance) commits without one. This only applies when capturing actuals at delivery — not during planning.
 ✓

What is a Product?

A Product is the physical goods specification — the record that holds the exact dimensions, mass, and loading behaviour of a specific item in your catalogue.

Think of a Product as a product datasheet — the physical specification for a specific goods item. Where a Product Group sets the rules (what can carry it, what variance is allowed), the Product holds the physical facts: how big it is, how heavy, how fast it loads, and whether anything can be stacked on top of it.

3D Visualiser requires physical dimensions. If you use the 3D Visualiser in the Planner, you must set the physical dimensions (Length, Width, Height) on each Product — the visualiser uses dimensions only, not mass. Without dimensions, the 3D Visualiser cannot represent the product visually. Mass is used separately, for Stackability calculations (see below).

Unlike Vehicle Classes and Site Classes, a Product can belong to more than one Product Group. A single product that is both refrigerated and hazardous can belong to both the "Frozen Goods" and "Hazardous Materials" groups — inheriting restrictions and attributes from both simultaneously.

Product Fields

Fields marked Required must be filled in before saving. Fields marked Recommended are not required by the system but are practically necessary for accurate planning.

Field Description Req Rec Opt
Name The display name of the product as it appears in orders and planning. ✓
Code Short unique identifier for this product. Used in imports, integrations, and order line items. ✓
Product Group(s) The group or groups this product belongs to. A product can belong to multiple groups — for example, both Frozen Goods and Hazardous Materials. Without a group, no shared restrictions or attributes apply to this product.
Recommended — products without a group will not inherit any handling restrictions or shared attributes.
 ✓
Size (Dimensions) The physical size of one unit of this product, entered against whichever Dimensions your organisation has configured in Settings (for example mass, volume, length, width, height, or pallets — size is not fixed to weight and volume). Used by the Planner to check vehicle capacity against the total size of a load.
If you use the 3D Visualiser, Length, Width, and Height must be set — the visualiser uses these dimensions only, not mass. If you use Stackability, Mass must also be set.
 ✓
Loading Rate How long it takes to load and offload this product, expressed in seconds per unit — LoadingSecondsPerUnit and OffloadingSecondsPerUnit. Used to calculate stop durations in the Planner.
This is not a fallback — the product, vehicle class, and site loading rates (wherever each is set) are all added together to produce the total stop duration.
 ✓
Stackability Controls whether other products can be stacked on top of this product. Set IsDisabled to prevent stacking entirely, or define maximum surface loads per orientation — Upright, LengthWise, and WidthWise — each with an optional AllowRotation flag and a MaxLoad mass limit. Used by the 3D Visualiser. ✓

Calculated Dimensions (specialised feature). Some environments also show auto-calculated fields — Volumetric Weight and Loading Metre Weight — which convert a bulky-but-light product's volume or loading metres into an equivalent mass, and a Chargeable Weight, which is the highest of these against the product's actual mass. These are calculated once a product is placed on an order (not set here) and are used for billing/costing purposes only — not for planning. This is a niche feature, not standard for most operations.

What is a Transport Unit?

A Transport Unit is a physical packaging or handling unit — a rolltainer, a hanging garment rail, a pallet — that takes up the same amount of space on a vehicle no matter how full it is. A rolltainer with a single item on it occupies the same truck space as a full one. This is different from a plain Product, whose footprint scales with the quantity loaded.

A Transport Unit can be packed with subline items, which are normally Products (nesting a transport unit inside another transport unit is technically possible but rarely used in practice). Outside of that, a Transport Unit is set up almost exactly like a Product — the same Size (Dimensions), Loading Rate, and Stackability fields apply.

There are really only two differences between a Transport Unit and a Product:

  • A Transport Unit can be packed with subline Products; a plain Product cannot.
  • A Transport Unit has a Capacity field describing how much can be packed inside it.
Capacity is not enforced. A Transport Unit's Capacity field is informational only — packing more into it than the stated capacity does not block planning, and may at most show a warning. Do not rely on it as a hard limit.

Adding a Product Group

Set up your Product Groups before adding products so you can assign products to groups during initial setup.

  1. 1

    Open Data Manager and go to Product Groups

    From the main navigation, open Data Manager. Select Product Groups from the entity list on the left. Click Add in the toolbar.

  2. 2

    Basic Info — enter a name

    Enter the group Name (required) — something that clearly describes the category, for example Frozen Goods, Bulk Liquids, or Hazardous Materials. The ID is auto-generated. The External ID is generated automatically too, from a template configured in Settings. Add a Description if needed. The Groups field assigns this class to a Group for shared-resource organisation — see Groups & Shared Resources for how this works. This is different from Settings → Partitions. Leave blank in a standard single-organisation setup.

  3. 3

    Specification — select an Icon and add shared Attributes

    Optionally select an Icon from the dropdown to visually identify this group in the interface. Add any Attributes that should be inherited by all products in this group — for example, a frozen_goods_product or Hazardous attribute used for matching in the Planner. If this is a catch-weight or bulk product category, also set the Variance Tolerance — choose a Kind (Value or Percentage) and set the acceptable Under and Over amounts.

  4. 4

    Attribute Rules — restrict which vehicles or sites can handle this group

    If only certain vehicle types or site types can handle products in this group, add the relevant Attribute Rules here. For example, if only refrigerated vehicles can carry frozen goods, set a rule that matches vehicles with a "refrigerated" attribute. Leave this tab blank if there are no handling restrictions.

  5. 5

    Click Commit

    Click Commit. The Product Group is now available to assign to products.

Adding a Product

Set up your Product Groups first so you can assign the product to a group during this process.

  1. 1

    Open Data Manager and go to Products

    From the main navigation, open Data Manager. Select Products from the entity list on the left. Click Add in the toolbar.

  2. 2

    Basic Info — enter the product name

    Enter the product Name (required). The ID is auto-generated. Add a Description if needed. The Groups field assigns this product to a Group for shared-resource organisation — see Groups & Shared Resources for how this works. Leave blank in a standard single-organisation setup.

  3. 3

    Specification → Links — assign Product Groups

    Select the Product Group or groups this product belongs to using the multi-select dropdown. A product can belong to more than one group — for example, a product that is both frozen and hazardous should be assigned to both groups simultaneously. If no group applies, leave this blank (but the product will not inherit any shared restrictions or attributes).

  4. 4

    Specification → Size — add dimension values

    Click the + button to add dimension rows. For each row, select the Dimension from the dropdown (the available dimensions match those configured in Settings — for example, mass, length, width, height), choose the Value Type, and enter the Value. Add one row per dimension you need to track for this product. Mass is the most important for capacity planning; length, width, and height are required for the 3D Visualiser (dimensions only — the visualiser does not use mass).

  5. 5

    Specification → Loading Rate — set loading and offloading times

    Select the Dimension Name from the dropdown — this identifies which unit dimension is the basis for loading time calculations (for example, the mass dimension). Enter the Loading seconds per unit and Off-loading seconds per unit. Leave at zero if loading time for this product does not differ from your other products.

  6. 6

    Specification → Stackability — set stacking rules

    Tick Stacking Disabled if nothing can be placed on top of this product. Leave unticked if stacking is permitted. The 3D Visualiser uses this setting when building a load plan.

  7. 7

    Click Commit

    Click Commit. The product is now available in the Planner and can be referenced in orders and load plans.

Tips & Common Mistakes

A few things to keep in mind when setting up Product Groups and Products for the first time.

  • Set up Product Groups before adding products. Even a single "General Goods" group is useful as a starting point for restrictions. Creating your groups first means you can assign products to them during initial setup rather than returning to each product later.
  • Enter dimensions carefully. The 3D Visualiser uses dimensions to draw the product in a load plan, so rough or approximate values lead to unrealistic load visualisations. Measure product dimensions from the outer packaging — the dimensions that matter are the space the product actually occupies in the vehicle.
  • A product can belong to multiple groups. Use this for products that have more than one set of handling requirements. A product assigned to both Frozen Goods and Hazardous Materials will inherit restrictions and attributes from both groups simultaneously — you do not need to duplicate rules.
  • Leaving dimensions blank when using the 3D Visualiser. If a product has no dimensions set, the 3D Visualiser cannot render it in the load plan — the product will simply not appear. This makes the visual load plan incomplete and unreliable. Always set Length, Width, and Height on every product you expect to see in the visualiser.
  • Setting a very tight Variance Tolerance on products that routinely vary in quantity. If the tolerance is narrower than the natural variation for a bulk or catch-weight product, the system will generate unnecessary warnings on deliveries. Set the tolerance to reflect the realistic variation you expect — not the theoretical ideal.
  • Forgetting that Loading Rate affects stop duration in the Planner. If a product takes a long time to load or unload and no loading rate is set anywhere — not on the product, the vehicle class, or the site — the Planner will underestimate stop times for orders that include this product. This leads to trips that run consistently late. If your products vary significantly in loading time, set the Loading Rate on the products that differ most from your average.
